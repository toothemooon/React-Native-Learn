## 2024-05-01 - Accessible Icon Buttons and Touch Targets
**Learning:** In React Native, `Pressable` components that only contain icons (like back buttons, settings gears, etc.) are completely opaque to screen readers by default. Screen reader users will simply hear "button" (if lucky) or just nothing. Furthermore, small icons without a `hitSlop` present a physical accessibility challenge for users with motor impairments or fat-finger errors on mobile devices.
**Action:** Always add `accessibilityRole="button"` and a descriptive `accessibilityLabel` to icon-only `Pressable` components. Always provide a generous `hitSlop` (e.g., `hitSlop={{ top: 10, bottom: 10, left: 10, right: 10 }}`) to expand the touch target area without affecting the visual layout.
## 2024-05-22 - Disabled State and Touch Target for Chat Send Buttons
**Learning:** In interactive chat interfaces (`ProfileScreen.tsx`), icon-only send buttons are prone to two UX issues: 1) they can be clicked when the input is empty (even if the click handler does nothing, the lack of visual feedback is confusing), and 2) they are hard to hit on mobile if the hit target matches the icon size exactly. In React Native, just setting `opacity: 0.5` doesn't communicate the disabled state to screen readers, and it doesn't prevent touch events.
**Action:** When implementing icon-only buttons for form submission or chat sending, always add: `disabled={true/false}`, `accessibilityRole="button"`, an `accessibilityLabel` (e.g., "发送消息"), `accessibilityState={{ disabled: true/false }}`, and crucially, a `hitSlop` prop (e.g. `hitSlop={{ top: 10, bottom: 10, left: 10, right: 10 }}`) to make it easier to tap reliably.
